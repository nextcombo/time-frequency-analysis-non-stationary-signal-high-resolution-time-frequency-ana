# Time-Frequency Analysis]Non-stationary Signal High-Resolution Time-Frequency Analysis Tool Based on Multi-Synchronization Compression [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Rotating machinery with bearing defects typically generates pulse signals that can be recorded by vibration sensors [1-7]. However, when the machine operates at variable speeds, signal processing becomes challenging. This is because the measured signals often exhibit strong non-stationary characteristics, meaning that the frequency of the signal significantly changes over time. Non-stationary signal analysis has gained increasing attention in the field of rotating machinery fault diagnosis. Time-frequency analysis (TFA) technology possesses strong capabilities to address these challenges. Several articles reviewing the development of TFA techniques in rotating machinery fault diagnosis can be found in [8-10]. It has been learned from recent research that energy concentration is a key indicator for evaluating the performance of TFA methods [11]. This is because concentrated TF representations (TFR) have better capability to characterize the fault features of potential signals. However, due to the limitations imposed by Heisenberg's uncertainty principle, traditional TFA methods such as Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), Wavelet Transform (WT), and S transform struggle to provide concentrated results when dealing with strong time-varying signals. In recent decades, various newly developed TFA techniques have attracted considerable attention aimed at overcoming the shortcomings of traditional methods. For example, reassignment method (RM) [12], synchronous compression transform (SST) [13,14], demodulation SST (DSST) [15,16], higher order SST [17-19], synchronous extraction transform (SET) [20,21] and multi-SST (MSST) [22].
The RM technique aims to sharpen the energy-blurred TFR by mapping data to a new set of coordinates that are closer to the central region based on the instantaneous frequency (IF) and local estimations of group delay [12]. This energy-blurred TFR is typically pre-generated using STFT, WT, or S transform. However, the mapping in RM is applied to the spectral or scale plots of signals, which are usually defined as square amplitudes of STFT or WT. This mapping loses the phase information of the signal, meaning that it cannot be reconstructed from the RM results. As a post-processing technique similar to RM, SST maps the TFR only based on the estimated values of IF, thus maintaining the ability for reverse processing. This makes SST more advantageous in many fields, such as mechanical fault diagnosis [23,24], seismic signal analysis [25,26], and respiratory dynamics analysis [27]. Recent studies have focused on further enhancing the capabilities of SST technology in characterizing strong time-varying signals [13-22].
SST technology is proposed within the framework of linear TFA algorithms, such as WT, STFT, and S transform. However, due to the defects of linear TF atoms, linear TFA methods cannot handle strong time-varying signals well. The corresponding TF results often suffer from blurred energy issues. SST also faces the same problem. Demodulation techniques design nonlinear TF atoms to characterize time-varying signals, effectively overcoming the problems of linear TF methods. Moreover, novel SST methods based on demodulation techniques show potential to improve energy concentration. This technique is commonly referred to as DSST method [15,16]. However, demodulation techniques must be designed based on prior information about the signal [9]. In practical applications, it is difficult or even impossible to determine the basic information of real-world signals in advance. This hinders the engineering application of DSST techniques.
The advanced SST method is proposed for processing high-frequency modulation (FM) signals, which does not require any prior information about the signal [17-19]. Theoretically, SST assumes that the analyzed signal should be purely harmonic. This means that SST is only suitable for addressing weak FM signals. To improve this situation, the framework of the higher-order SST is built on more complex signal models, such as linear FM signals and higher-order polynomial FM signals. In the absence of noise, the higher-order SST can provide highly concentrated results for strong time-varying signals. However, recent studies have found that the higher-order SST method is very sensitive to noise. For signals with larger noise, using this method often leads to unsatisfactory results [22].
The SET method is designed to retain only the TF coefficients closely related to the time-varying characteristics of the signal [20, 21]. Moreover, SET eliminates most of the weakly correlated TF coefficients. Therefore, the SET result is more concentrated than the SST result. However, SET only provides an approximate reconstruction of the signal. The performance of reconstruction decreases with increasing nonlinearity of the signal.
A recently published paper introduces the MSST method, which uses an iterative process to improve the energy concentration of SST. MSST allows perfect reconstruction of the signal and does not require a priori information. [22] shows that in handling both noisy and noisy signals, MSST can provide more concentrated results than RM, DSST, and higher-order SST. The MSST method seems to be a promising tool for achieving ideal TFR (ITFR). However, [22]'s discussion section points out a major issue in the MSST method, namely the non-redistribution point problem, which hinders the concentrated representation of TF features.
This paper first presents a detailed theorem on the unresolvable point problem in MSST methods, and then proposes an improved MSST method that can effectively solve this problem. This new method still allows for perfect signal reconstruction and exhibits good robustness against noise. It does not require additional parameters or prior information about the signal, and has limited computational costs. The structure of this paper is as follows.
